# Outward 2 Early Access Date Confirmed: February 8 on Steam, Epic, and GOG

Nine Dots Studio has officially announced that **Outward 2 will launch in Early Access on February 8** across Steam, Epic Games Store, and GOG. The announcement came alongside a new trailer, giving the community its clearest look yet at what the sequel has in store.

For fans who have been watching this one for a while, having a concrete date is a significant milestone. Outward 2 has been a long-anticipated follow-up to the cult-hit survival RPG, which built a devoted fanbase despite — or arguably because of — its unforgiving systems and refusal to hold your hand.

---

## What We Know About the Release

The simultaneous launch across Steam, Epic, and GOG is worth noting. GOG's inclusion in particular will be welcome news for players who prefer DRM-free copies, a detail that aligns with the original Outward's availability on that platform. No pricing has been confirmed in the materials available at time of writing, but Early Access titles on all three storefronts typically carry a discount relative to their planned full-release price.

The accompanying trailer released alongside the date announcement, though a full breakdown of its content is still being compiled as footage circulates. We'll update this piece as more specific mechanical details surface from the trailer.

**Confirmed facts so far:**
- Early Access launch date: **February 8**
- Platforms: **Steam, Epic Games Store, GOG** (PC only at this stage)
- Developer: **Nine Dots Studio**
- A new trailer has been released alongside the announcement

---

## What Returning Players Are Hoping to See

The original Outward was, as many players put it, a "rough gem" — a game with genuine mechanical depth and atmosphere that also had real friction points. A recent Reddit thread on r/outwardgame captures this tension well: a player returning after years away noted they loved the core of the game but found themselves grinding through a handful of persistent problems.

The issues most commonly raised in community discussions like this one include:

**Excessive backtracking and travel time.** The original game required a lot of walking — intentionally so, as travel is part of the survival experience. But without any fast travel or waypoint options, even veteran players found that a lot of playtime was spent simply moving between locations rather than engaging with the game's systems. Whether Outward 2 will introduce any traversal shortcuts is currently unconfirmed.

**Static world and thin NPC presence.** A recurring criticism of Outward 1 was that towns felt hollow. NPCs had limited dialogue, no real schedules or sense of agency, and the world didn't react meaningfully to player actions. This is the kind of thing that's difficult to fix with DLC — it requires foundational changes to how the game is built. Whether Nine Dots have addressed this in the sequel is something the Early Access period will reveal.

**Quest volume and variety.** The original had a relatively small quest pool, and the dungeon design — while atmospheric — was considered crude in terms of layout and encounter design. Players hoping for Outward 2 to deliver a denser, more varied set of missions and locations have reason to be cautiously optimistic, but nothing specific has been confirmed.

> **Note:** The points above are drawn from community feedback (specifically r/outwardgame discussions) and represent player expectations, not confirmed features of Outward 2. Treat them as a wishlist, not a feature list.

## Should You Play Outward 1 (with DLC) or Wait for Outward 2?

This is a question that's coming up frequently right now, and the honest answer depends on your situation.

**If you haven't played Outward at all:**  
The original game with its DLC — The Soroboreans and The Three Brothers — offers a substantial amount of content and represents the most polished version of the first game's vision. Both expansions add new regions, faction questlines, and mechanical tweaks that address some (though not all) of the base game's rougher edges. If you're curious about the series, playing through Outward 1 + DLC before the sequel launches in February is a reasonable way to spend the next few months.

**If you played at launch and bounced off it:**  
The DLC does improve the experience, but it doesn't fundamentally overhaul the systems that the original playerbase found limiting. If your issues were primarily with the static world, limited quest variety, or the travel grind, the expansions won't fully solve those. In that case, waiting for Outward 2 — which presumably builds from the ground up with lessons learned — may be the better call.

**If you're already a fan and just want more:**  
Play the DLC now if you haven't. It's good content, and it'll put you in a stronger position to appreciate what Outward 2 changes or keeps when Early Access arrives.

## What Early Access Means for Outward 2

Nine Dots Studio going the Early Access route is an interesting choice that has implications for how you should approach the launch in February.

Early Access for an RPG of this type usually means the game is playable and contains a meaningful chunk of content, but is missing systems, regions, or story elements that will be added during the EA period. For survival RPGs in particular, balance is often rough early on — expect the economy, stamina/hunger/thirst curves, and combat numbers to shift during the EA phase as player feedback rolls in.

The upside is that Nine Dots will be able to tune the experience in response to real player behavior rather than internal testing alone. The original Outward benefited from community feedback post-launch, and getting that input earlier in Outward 2's lifecycle could result in a better 1.0 release.

If you're the type of player who wants a complete, polished experience, it may be worth waiting for full release. If you want to be part of shaping the game and don't mind rougher edges, February 8 is your date.
